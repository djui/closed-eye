Closed-Eye

What you see with your eyes shut is not darkness. It is the visual system looking at itself: filtered light, spontaneous nerve fire, and debris drifting in a slow internal sea.

Closed-eye colours

Daylight reaching a closed eye is strained through skin, the tarsal plate and a dense mesh of eyelid blood. Haemoglobin absorbs shorter wavelengths, so the field warms from pale sky-blue into green, yellow, orange and a deep retinal red. In true darkness the red recedes, leaving eigenlicht — the faint blue-grey glow of the retina’s own activity.

Phosphenes and retinal noise

Photoreceptors and ganglion cells fire without photons. The brain reads this dark noise as scintillation: countless irregular points that flicker, drift and die, never a frozen grain. Pressure on the globe, a shift of attention, or the edge of sleep can swell these phosphenes into clouds. The slider here stands in for that pressure — and for how much light the lids admit.

Vitreous floaters

The vitreous humour is a gel of water, collagen and hyaluronan. With time and motion it condenses into fibres, loops and cobwebs (muscae volitantes). They hang in a fluid that does not rotate with the eye at once, so a glance sends them lagging, overshooting, then coasting. Very weak gravity slowly settles them toward the lower field.

Optical defocus

Floaters lie well in front of the retina. Their shadows are therefore optically defocused: a dark core with a faint inverted halo — the point-spread function of a slightly myopic optical system, not a double image or an offset copy.

Inertia and the almost-dream

Closed eyes still move: microsaccades, slow drifts, quiet pauses. Without a sensor, this piece invents that rhythm. Swipe or drag to suggest a glance; on a phone, motion can stand in for the turning globe; a camera, if you allow it, can follow the eyes themselves. Control is never complete. The field has its own viscosity.

Faced with unstructured light, perception supplies weather, faces, meaning. The same machinery that will later spin dreams is already at work here, interpreting the noise of looking inward.
